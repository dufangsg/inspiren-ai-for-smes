December Will Be Busy. Your Headcount Won't Be.

Retail sales are barely growing, rents and wages are not waiting, and the year-end rush still needs staffing. Singapore's refreshed retail plan now sorts AI tools by where the hours go. Here is where to start.

26 November 2026 · 6-minute read

Every retailer knows the next five weeks. More customers, more stock moving, more messages on WhatsApp and Instagram, more returns in January. What does not grow is the team. The same people who open the shop are the ones answering chats at midnight and recounting stock on Sunday.

And the money is not making it easier. Retail sales in September 2026 were only 1.7% higher than a year earlier, and lower than in August once seasonal effects are removed. Department stores and convenience stores sold less than a year before. About 17.7% of sales now happen online, so the competition is not only the shop next door.

Most retailers have the basics. Few have gone further.

This is not a sector that ignores technology. According to the refreshed Retail Industry Digital Plan, launched in May by Enterprise Singapore and IMDA, over 75% of retail SMEs already use entry-level digital solutions and 45% use intermediate ones. Advanced adoption, the government says, remains limited.

In other words: the POS is there, the online shop is there. What is missing is the layer that takes work off people's hands.

The new plan sorts AI tools by where the work happens

The old plan was a ladder: foundational, intermediate, advanced. The refreshed plan drops that and groups solutions by business touchpoint instead. That is a more useful way to think, because it starts from your day, not from the technology.

It targets over 2,000 SME retailers, and IMDA's 2025 survey found 88% of retail enterprises keen to adopt at least one sector-specific solution in 2026. Being keen is not the same as starting, though. The practical question is which box to open first.

What it looked like for one retailer

The plan's own case study is Far East Flora. Self-checkout cut cashier workload by an estimated 30 to 40%. Generative AI cut the time to produce marketing content by about half. An AI marketing tool raised returns on the same spend by an estimated 5 to 10%. Staff training moved to 10 to 15 minute modules on their phones.

These are one company's own estimates, not averages. Your numbers will differ. What is worth copying is the pattern: each tool was aimed at one specific chunk of staff time.

How to pick your first one

  1. Find the hours, not the tool. For one week, note where staff time actually goes: answering the same product questions, writing posts and listings, counting and reordering stock, training new part-timers.
  2. Match the biggest chunk to a box. Repeated customer questions point to front of house. Stock-outs and overstock point to demand forecasting. Content and training point to the corporate tools.
  3. Check the list and the money. Pre-approved retail solutions are listed on IMDA's SMEs Go Digital platform. Funding now comes through the EDGE grant, where up to S$30,000 a year can go to digital solutions. Apply before you pay any deposit.
  4. Do not skip the fourth box. A chatbot that holds customer data needs basic protection: anti-malware, firewall and backup are in the plan for a reason.

The question for January

The year-end rush will end. The staffing problem will not. The retailers who come out of it in better shape will not be the ones with the newest technology. They will be the ones who know which hour of their team's week they are trying to win back.

If you could hand one task in your shop to AI before Chinese New Year, which would it be?

Your Kitchen Has Five Systems and None of Them Talk to Each Other

Six in ten F&B operators run mostly standalone digital tools. A new programme will fund up to half the cost of connecting them, and adding AI on top.

24 November 2026 · 6-minute read

Many F&B owners in Singapore have already gone digital. There is often a POS at the counter, an ordering system for stock, a roster app, and a tablet for each delivery platform. Each one works. The trouble is that they do not work with each other.

So someone copies the day's sales into a spreadsheet, re-types the stock count into an order, and answers the same "how many do we have left?" question on WhatsApp. That is not a technology shortage. It is a connection shortage, and it eats hours that a thin team does not have.

The numbers behind it

A UOB survey of Singapore F&B businesses in June 2026 found that six in ten operators have most of their digital solutions running as standalone systems. About one in three has yet to adopt AI tools at all.

In July, Enterprise Singapore (EnterpriseSG) and UOB launched a programme aimed at exactly that gap. It targets at least 200 F&B businesses over the next two years, helping them move from separate systems to integrated, AI-enabled operations.

What the programme actually offers

  • Advice first. Eligible businesses get advisory support from UOB's SME Banking team to identify suitable solutions.
  • Preferential rates. AI-enabled solutions and integration services are available from a panel of providers at programme rates.
  • Funding. EnterpriseSG provides up to 50% funding for SMEs, and up to 30% for larger firms.
  • Workshops. EnterpriseSG and UOB will also run joint capability-building workshops on system integration and AI use.

The initial provider panel includes OpenMinds, which connects disconnected systems to cut manual data entry; PayBoy, whose GutsyHQ software links standalone systems and offers AI assistants over chat apps such as WhatsApp for sales, stock and manpower questions; and VisionTech, which builds AI solutions for F&B. More providers may be added.

Who qualifies

According to UOB's programme page, you need to be registered and operating in Singapore, with food and beverage service (SSIC 56) as a primary or secondary activity. At least 30% of shareholding must be held by Singapore citizens or permanent residents, and you need a UOB business account.

An SME here means group annual sales of no more than S$100 million, or no more than 200 employees. You are not eligible if you previously received grant support for a similar solution. A grant for a different project does not count against you.

How to apply, and the date that matters

You do not apply to EnterpriseSG yourself. You pick a pre-approved provider, and the provider submits the grant application for you. You can choose only one provider for the integration work, and each UEN needs its own application.

The deadline is the one to plan around: you must sign up with a provider, implement the solutions and complete payment by 30 June 2027. Leave time for implementation. A project signed in June will not make it.

What to decide before you call anyone

Funding can make a bad project cheaper, but it cannot make it a good one. Before the first consultation, write down three things:

  1. The two systems your team spends the most time copying between.
  2. The question you ask staff most often that a system should answer, such as stock left, today's sales or who is on shift.
  3. The number of hours a week that copying costs you now. This is your baseline.

The UOB page does not state a dollar cap on the funding, and it says terms and conditions apply, so confirm the full terms with the programme partners before you sign.

The question for 2027

The kitchen will not get any easier to run. Rent, wages and ingredient costs are not waiting for you. The owners who free up an hour a day will not be the ones who bought another tool. They will be the ones who got the tools they already have to work together.

This article is general information, not financial or legal advice. Programme terms may change.

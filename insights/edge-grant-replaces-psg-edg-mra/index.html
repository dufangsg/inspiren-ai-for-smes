Three Grants Just Became One. Most SMEs Haven't Noticed.

PSG, EDG and MRA are gone. EDGE replaced them on 30 September. Here is what changed, what did not, and the 31 March date that decides whether you use this year's money.

25 November 2026 · 6-minute read

For years, the first question any Singapore SME asked about a new system was: is this PSG or EDG? Off-the-shelf tool, PSG. Bigger custom project, EDG. Going overseas, MRA. Three schemes, three sets of rules, and plenty of time spent working out which one applied.

That question no longer exists. On 30 September 2026, Enterprise Singapore replaced all three with a single grant called EDGE. The old schemes stopped taking new applications the day before.

What actually changed

EDGE covers more than 150 activities across eight business areas. Instead of picking a scheme, you pick an activity and apply for it. Each activity is its own application, and several can run at the same time.

The biggest change is the cap. Each company now has one annual cap of S$100,000 across everything it applies for. The cap year runs from 1 April to 31 March and resets every year.

Within that cap, up to S$30,000 can go to single-function digital solutions, integrated enterprise systems such as ERP, and pre-scoped equipment. Single-function digital solutions and integrated systems are limited to one application per company.

Support levels are familiar. SMEs get up to 50%, and up to 70% for internationalisation and sustainability activities. Larger firms get up to 30%, or 50% for those two areas. To qualify, a business must be registered in Singapore with at least 30% Singaporean or PR ownership. An SME means group sales of no more than S$100 million, or no more than 200 staff.

What did not change, and catches people out

You still cannot start before you apply. If work has begun, or any payment or deposit has been made before you submit, the application counts as retrospective and is not supported. This is the most common way SMEs lose a grant they would otherwise have received.

It is still a reimbursement. You pay the vendor in full first. The grant comes back after the project is done and you submit one final claim.

Two smaller points are new. Vendors can no longer submit the application for you; only staff with the right Corppass role can. And past PSG, EDG or MRA grants do not count against your EDGE cap, but EDGE will not fund a project those schemes already supported.

The dates that matter

If you had an application in before 30 September, it continues under the old scheme's rules. For everything new, these dates decide your timing.

The one to watch is 31 March 2027. To use this cap year, submit before then. Miss it, and the project draws on next year's S$100,000 instead.

How to plan an AI project under EDGE

The single cap changes how you should plan. Under the old system, a digital tool and an overseas push came from different pots. Now they share one. So decide the order before you apply:

  1. Pick the process first, not the tool. Choose the task that costs your team the most hours: quoting, invoicing, stock, scheduling, customer replies. Write down how many hours a week it takes today.
  2. Check the S$30,000 digital limit. If the fix is an off-the-shelf AI tool, it sits inside that limit. Larger AI and process redesign work may fit other activities under the S$100,000.
  3. Use the official finders. Pre-approved single-function solutions are listed on IMDA's SMEs Go Digital platform. The new BizSG portal has an AI grant recommender that takes a plain-language description of your plan.
  4. Apply, wait, then sign. Do not pay a deposit or let the vendor start until the application is in.

The question for the next four months

EDGE makes the money easier to find. It does not make the project easier to choose. The SMEs that get the most from it will not be the ones that apply first. They will be the ones that know which hour of work they are trying to win back before they talk to a vendor.

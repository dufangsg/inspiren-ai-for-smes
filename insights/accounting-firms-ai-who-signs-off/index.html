Your Staff Already Use AI Every Week. Who Signs Off on It?

Eight in ten accounting firms in Singapore and Malaysia now use generative AI weekly. Fewer than four in ten have written down the rules. For a small practice, that gap is the real risk, and the easiest one to close.

27 November 2026 · 6-minute read

Ask a small accounting or corporate services firm whether it uses AI, and the partner may say "not really". Ask the staff, and you get a different answer. They are drafting emails with it, summarising contracts, explaining tax rules, cleaning up working papers.

A new study from Wolters Kluwer, published on 18 November, puts numbers on it. Among 271 accounting firms in Malaysia and Singapore, 82% use generative AI daily or weekly. Only 38% have a formal, written, firm-wide AI policy.

The work is moving, and fast

ACRA's Accounting Entities Survey, released on 11 November, shows why this matters now. Sector revenue grew 5.1% in 2025, to about S$3.7 billion. But the growth was uneven.

Business advisory grew 12.9%. Corporate support, the routine filing and secretarial work, fell 7.4%. Micro accounting entities saw revenue drop 6.2%, while small, medium and large firms grew.

Routine work is exactly what AI does well, and clients know it. The value is shifting to judgement and advice. Firms that use AI to clear the routine and spend the time on advisory are the ones growing.

Speed is not the risk. Unchecked output is.

The same survey shows firms know where the danger lies. Accuracy and verification is the top concern, at 46%, followed by privacy and security at 40% and lack of staff AI experience at 36%.

The firms that have set rules are serious about review. Among those with a written policy or general guidance, 63% require every AI-generated technical output to be reviewed and signed off by a qualified professional. Another 35% require review of high-risk or client-facing work.

As Wolters Kluwer's Asia Pacific head put it, AI can help with research and analysis, but professional accountability cannot be delegated to technology. The signature on the report is still yours.

A one-page AI policy for a small firm

You do not need a governance committee. You need one page that every staff member has read. It should answer five questions:

Write it this week, walk the team through it in 20 minutes, and review it every six months as the tools change.

Then put the time back into advisory

ACRA's survey also asked firms which skills they most want to build. AI application, ethics and governance came first, at 67%. More than 34,000 professionals and students have enrolled in the AI Fluency Programme run by ISCA and IMDA.

Skills matter, but the order matters more. Rules first, so the team uses AI safely. Then training, so they use it well. Then the hours saved go where the growth is: helping clients make decisions, not just filing for them.
